Aeronautical Engineering Course Overview

Aeronautical engineering course is perfect for candidates interested in designing, developing, researching, constructing, maintaining, or renovating flying objects like spacecraft and aircraft. Those who are interested in this field can enrol themselves in diploma, undergraduate, postgraduate, or PhD courses. Online courses are also available in this field. Aeronautical engineering covers the study of aerodynamic elements of aircraft. 

The aeronautical engineering syllabus and subjects include aerodynamics, thermodynamics, aircraft maintenance, etc. Students of this course can learn to design and maintain helicopters, aeroplanes, jet machines, military aircraft, jet machines, and satellites. The aeronautical engineering course duration is 3 years for a diploma course, 4 years for an undergraduate course (BTech Aeronautical Engineering), 2 years for PG (MTech Aeronautical Engineering), and 3-6 years for a PhD. 

Interested candidates should check out if they are eligible for the aeronautical engineering courses after 12th. The eligibility requirements involve clearing Class 12th with Physics, Chemistry, and Mathematics (PCM) as compulsory subjects and a minimum of 60% aggregate. They must also check the entrance exams accepted by universities and institutions for admission. The aeronautical engineering entrance exams for admission include AME CET, JEE Advanced, JEE Main, GATE, LPUNEST, etc.

While the fees for this course range from INR 5-10 lakhs, the aeronautical engineering salary starts from INR 6 Lakhs per annum and goes up with experience. Additionally, there’s a rising demand for aircraft and aircraft engineers in and around India. Thus, the return on investment and future prospects of this course are good. On this page, aspiring candidates can find all the details they need to pursue an aeronautical engineering course.

FAQs

Q1. What is the difference between aerospace and aeronautical engineering?

Aerospace engineering is the study of all spacecraft used within and outside of the earth's atmosphere, whereas aeronautical engineering is the study of aeroplanes that operate inside the earth's atmosphere. It focuses on developing both aircraft and spacecraft.

Q2. What do aeronautical engineers do?

Engineers who work with aircraft are known as aeronautical engineers. They are largely involved in the design of aircraft and propulsion systems, as well as the research of aircraft and construction materials' aerodynamic performance. They study the science, technology, and practice of flight within the atmosphere of the Earth.

Q3. Does NASA hire aeronautical engineers?

Yes, NASA hires aeronautical engineers who are well-qualified and trained.

Directorate General of Civil Aviation: The Directorate General of Civil Aviation, or DGCA as it is called, is an agency of the Ministry of Civil Aviation that controls civil aviation. It is responsible for the regulation of air transport services, the enforcement of civil aviation legislation, air safety, and airworthiness standards.

Hindustan Aeronautics Ltd (HAL): A state-owned aerospace and defence business overseen by the Ministry of Defence, HAL is responsible for the design, production, development, repair, and overhaul of aeroplanes and helicopters. Its services also include engines and related equipment like avionics, instrumentation, and accessories.

Indian Air Force: The Indian Air Force is the Indian Armed Forces' air arm. Its complement of people and aircraft assets ranks fourth among the world's air forces. Its major role is to protect Indian airspace and perform aerial combat during times of war.

CSIR National Aeronautics Lab (NAL): The Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) of India includes the National Aerospace Laboratories (NAL). It creates aeronautical technology and designs for small and medium-sized civil aircraft.

Aeronautical Development Establishment (ADE): The Aeronautical Development Establishment (ADE) is a laboratory of the Defence Research and Development Organization (DRDO) that is responsible for the design and development of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles, as well as Aeronautical Systems and technologies. These cutting-edge vehicles and equipment will be used by the Indian Armed Forces.

Ministry of Civil Aviation: The Ministry of Civil Aviation is a government agency. The Directorate General of Civil Aviation, the Bureau of Civil Aviation Security, and the Indira Gandhi Rashtriya Udan Academy are working under the Ministry of Civil Aviation. The ministry also oversees affiliated Public Sector Undertakings such Airports Authority of India (AAI), National Aviation Company of India Ltd, and Pawan Hans Helicopters Limited. 

Private Airlines: Private airlines are privately owned by a person or group of individuals in partnership.

Courses After Aeronautical Engineering Course

Candidates after finishing BTech aeronautical engineering course have various career pathways to choose from. Following are the options to choose from after studying aeronautical engineering. 

Pursue a Master’s Degree

After studying BTech aeronautical engineering degree, candidates can go for higher education and pursue an MTech in aeronautical engineering or a PhD. Higher education can give more in-depth information, research possibilities, and pathways to higher careers in academia or R&D.

Start Working Professionally

After finishing the aeronautical engineering course, you can start working immediately. Many recruiters offer jobs to B Tech aeronautical engineering graduates. You can work in the Air Force, Army, NASA, ISRO, or even in private airline companies. 

Join Defence Sector

If you have a passion do something for the nation but you believe that by pursuing an engineering degree you won’t contribute to that, you are wrong. You can work in the defence sector and contribute to national safety by designing and developing military aircraft, drones, or missile systems. This industry provides difficult and high-tech projects with an emphasis on national security.

Join Space Industry

If you are interested in being part of space crafts and exploring space, then you can join the space industry after studying aeronautical engineering and working in NASA and ISRO. Though cracking a job in NASA and ISRO is tough, you can get it if you are qualified and skilled. You may work on satellite design and manufacture, space exploration missions, or spacecraft and launch vehicle development.

Start Your Own Business and Be an Entrepreneur

Starting your aerospace-related firm or joining a startup in the sector might be an exciting choice if you love innovation and want to bring your ideas to life. However, it demands significant funding.
