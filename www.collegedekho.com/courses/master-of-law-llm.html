LLM Overview

LLM full form is Master of Laws (LL.M/ L.L.M/ M.L./ Latin Legum Magister). LLM is a postgraduate law course in India that typically lasts for one to two years. During this course students have the opportunity to delve deeply into any specific area of law through research and studies. LLM degree can only be pursued by candidates who have mandatorily completed their LLB or Bachelor of Laws degree from a recognized institute in India. LLM Syllabus covers major topics like Constitutional Law, Law of Torts, Law of Contracts, Criminal Law, Administrative Law among others.

To be eligible for admission to an LLM course in India individuals must hold a bachelor's degree in law (LLB) with a score of 55 per cent or higher. Some renowned institutions offering LLM courses include NLSIU, Symbiosis Law School, NLU Delhi, NALSAR and NUJS. Aspiring candidates are required to pass law entrance exams such as AILET, CLAT PG, LSAT, DU LLM, AP PGLCET, etc.

Pursuing an LLM not only opens up opportunities for a career but also allows individuals to specialise in various fields of law such as Criminal Law, Business Law, Administrative Law, Environmental Law, Cyber Law and more. The structured nature of the LLM programme provides knowledge that aligns with the career goals of the students.

There are some wonderful LLM Jobs out there once you complete your degree, such as Legal Tech Specialist, Corporate Lawyer, Criminal Lawyer, Law Professor, etc. The average annual LLM Salary is also quite competitive; annual pay package for freshers starts from Rs. 4,50,000 and can go up to Rs. 7,00,000.

Why Choose an LLM Degree?

If your career aspirations or personal life requires it, you may decide to take an LLM degree in India. Here are some key factors to consider while you’re thinking about pursuing an LLM course in India:

Upgrading Career Prospects:

Specialisation: An LLM allows you to focus on the subfields of law such as human rights or business laws in more detail. It can focus on areas such as environmental legislations and business policies. Law degree can delve into further details of any given legal topic than normal classes would. Such job applicants can focus on a particular area of law and become more interesting to potential employers that may employ them.
Improved Skills: Doing LLM will make you improve in the essential critical skills such as research and writing that are necessary in a good law career position. You will also get an opportunity to learn by doing things such as research projects, internships and participation in mock court proceedings. This is in addition to your class lessons in schools or colleges.
Networking: Having friends with teachers, other students and graduates from the law field in India can contribute greatly to your career. There are many LLM schools in India that have a strong alumni base. Alumni members can provide mentorship and job opportunities after graduation.

Advantages of studying in India:

Cost-effectiveness: Not only pursuing LLM in India is more cost effective than pursuing LLM abroad, it also helps students to understand the Indian legal system in a better way and practice law in India.
Language flexibility: In India, you can practice law in Hindi and all other local languages and there is no compulsion of fighting legal cases only in English.

Other factors to consider:

Course reputation: A good LLM degree from an accredited institution can do wonders for your legal career. It will not only enhance the reputation of your degree but will also demonstrate the potential employers how high skilled you are.
Faculty expertise: There are some of the best and learned legal faculty in the world at reputed Indian law institutes. To acquire all your information concerning the law from this honorable and learned group of people will help you a lot in becoming a good legal practitioner.

Is an Online LLM degree available in India?

No, LLM degrees offered online in India usually are not accepted by the Bar Council of India (BCI) that rules over legal education and practice. This means you can't use that degree to act as a lawyer in Indian courts. Some schools in other countries offer online LLM classes. These might be accepted back where they are from, but it is not acceptable in India. These online LLM classes can still be helpful for learning things about a certain part of law, but you would not use that diploma alone to practice law in India.

Types of LLM Courses

There are broadly two types of LLM courses available in India:

1. Full-time LLM:

  • Duration: Typically 1-2 years, depending on the program and specialisation.
  • Structure: Intensive coursework, seminars, research projects, and internships.
  • Benefits: Immersive learning experience, networking opportunities, and potential for full-time job placements during or after the program.
  • Popular full-time LLM programmes: Business Law, Corporate Law, Intellectual Property Law, Taxation Law, Criminal Law, Environmental Law, Human Rights Law, etc.

2. Part-time LLM:

  • Duration: Typically 2-3 years, with evening or weekend classes.
  • Structure: Flexible schedule for working professionals, often with a mix of online and offline components.
  • Benefits: Continue working while gaining advanced legal knowledge, upskilling opportunities, and improved career prospects.
  • Popular part-time LLM programmes: Business Law, Corporate Law, Taxation Law, Labour Law, etc.

LLM Eligibility Criteria

LLM course eligibility criteria varies from one institute to another, however, here's the general LLM eligibility requirements that are common across various colleges/ universities.

  • Educational Qualification: According to 5-year LLB courses, you need to have 10+2 from an acknowledged school in India OR if you have done 3-year LLB program from a UGC recognized college/ university after your graduation.
  • Minimum Aggregate Marks: In most cases, many schools require a minimum number of marks for a specific LLM degree. This usually falls between 50% and 60% and lower aggregate marks are permitted for such groups as SC/ST or other reserved groups.
  • Entrance Exams: Most schools and colleges hold exams in order to admit you to their LLC programme. Some use national-level tests like CLAT PG, AILET, LSAT India or SLS AIAT. Other institutes have their own institute-level law exams other than national-level or state-level law exams.

Additional Eligibility Requirements

  • Work Experience: A few colleges might prefer candidates with relevant work experience in the legal field.
  • Research Proposal: Some law institutes require a research proposal for entry into specific LLM courses.
  • Personal Interview: Some universities might conduct a personal interview as part of the selection process. These are generally those law institutes that hire on merit basis or via institute-level law entrance exam.

LLM Entrance Exams

There are multiple entrance exams for LLM in India, but some of the most popular ones are:

Common Law Admission Test (CLAT): This is a national-level exam conducted by the Consortium of National Law Universities (NLUs) for admission to postgraduate law programs in 22 NLUs and other participating universities.

All India Law Entrance Test (AILET): This is another national-level exam conducted by the National Law University, Delhi (NLU Delhi) for admission to its LLM programme.

Law School Admission Test (LSAT): This is an international standardised test conducted by the Law School Admission Council (LSAC) for admission to law schools in India and around the world.

Delhi University LLM Entrance Test (DU LLM): This is a university-level exam conducted by the University of Delhi for admission to its LLM programme.

TISS National Entrance Test (TISSNET): This is a national-level exam conducted by the Tata Institute of Social Sciences (TISS) for admission to its postgraduate programs, including LLM.

SLS AIAT Exam: The Symbiosis Law School All India Admission Test (SLS AIAT) is an entrance exam for admission to postgraduate LLM courses at Symbiosis International University. It's a gateway to LLM programmes at SLS Pune, Hyderabad, Nagpur, and Noida. The exam is conducted online in a home-based remote proctored mode and tests your legal aptitude, reasoning, writing skills, and current affairs knowledge. 

Besides CLAT, AILET, and LSAT India, other popular LLM entrance exam options include PU CET for Punjab universities, IPU CET for Delhi NCR, and state-level exams like AP PGLCET and TS PGLCET.

LLM Entrance Exams Syllabus 

LLM entrance exam syllabuses can differ depending on the specific entrance test you're taking, but they generally cover a range of legal, logical, and general knowledge topics. Here's a breakdown of common sections that you might encounter in these LLM entrance exams:

Legal Aptitude & Reasoning:

  • Legal Reasoning: Analyse legal principles, identify relevant facts, and draw logical conclusions.
  • Contract Law: Understand basic principles of contracts and their formation, breach, and remedies.
  • Constitutional Law: Knowledge of the Indian Constitution, fundamental rights, and judicial review.
  • Torts & Criminal Law: Grasp key concepts in torts (civil wrongs) and criminal law, including liability and defences.

Analytical Reasoning & Logical Deduction:

  • Critical Reasoning: Evaluate arguments, identify assumptions, and draw inferences.
  • Data Interpretation: Analyse graphs, charts, and tables to extract key information.
  • Logical Reasoning: Identify patterns, solve puzzles, and make logical deductions.

General Knowledge & Current Affairs:

  • Static GK: History, Geography, Indian Polity, Economics, and Science.
  • Current Affairs: Knowledge of recent national and international events, legal developments, and social issues.

English Language & Comprehension:

  • Reading Comprehension: Read and understand passages from various sources.
  • Vocabulary: Demonstrate a strong understanding of English vocabulary.
  • Grammar & Usage: Apply correct grammar and usage in written English.

LLM Entrance Quick Preparation Tips 

Here are some section-wise quick preparation tips for LLM entrance exams:

Legal Reasoning Section:

  • Link important legal principles (contracts, torts, constitution) for easier recall and better comprehension.
  • Make pocket revision easier by condensing the crucial legal terms and principles onto flashcards.
  • Critically analyze practice questions. Critique assumptions, break down arguments, and strengthen your analytical thinking prowess.

Logical and Analytical Reasoning Section:

  • Refine your critical thinking skills with online practice tests that resemble the format of a test.
  • Participate in discussions in a friendly way regarding current issues to develop better critical thinking and argument. The mental arguments will set you up for the analytical challenges of the test.
  • Inject a little logic fun in your daily schedule with sudoku puzzles. They will improve your pattern recognition and deduction abilities.

General Knowledge Section:

  • Get updated with current headlines from reliable sources on a daily basis regarding important legal, social, and political issues
  • Spend 15 minutes per day on a particular GK topic (history, economics etc.) to accumulate GK regularly.
  • Employ online quizzes for testing your general knowledge and to determine what needs to be improved.

English Comprehension Section:

  • Read diverse reading materials such as news articles, legal journals, and fiction to boost vocabulary and comprehension skills.
  • Journal or blog regularly to improve your grammar, sentence structure, and writing fluency.
  • Revitalize your vocabulary through board games such as Scrabble or crossword puzzles. They will keep your word recall keen and as fit as possible.

With the smart application of these effective tactics, you can perfect your LLM entrance exam preparation and obtain the desired results.

LLM Admission Process in India

While the specific admission process steps might differ slightly between universities and entrance exams, the general LLM admission process in India follows these crucial stages:

1.Check the Eligibility Requirements:

  • Ensure you hold a Bachelor's degree in Law (LLB) from a recognized university.
  • Meet the minimum percentage requirement (usually 50% for general, 45% for reserved categories).
  • Some universities might have additional criteria like work experience or specific LLB specialisation. Check the same on the university/ college official website.

2.  Application and Registration:

  • Register for the chosen entrance exam within the specified timeframe on the official website.
  • Fill out the online application forms carefully, providing accurate information and attaching required documents.
  • Pay the application and entrance exam fees as applicable.

3. Attend the required Entrance Exam:

  • Choose the relevant entrance exam based on your target universities and programme focus (CLAT, AILET, LSAT India, etc.).
  • Familiarise yourself with the exam syllabus, format, and question types.
  • Utilise study materials like textbooks, mock tests, and online resources to better prepare for your LLM entrance test.
  • Take the entrance exam on the scheduled date, following the designated test centre and guidelines.

4. Results and Merit List:

  • Check the official website for the exam results announcement date.
  • Analyse your performance and see if you secured a rank within the acceptable range for your desired universities.
  • Universities will publish merit lists based on entrance exam scores and other selection criteria.

5. Counselling and Seat Allotment:

  • Attend counselling sessions or participate in online seat allotment processes as per the university instructions.
  • Choose your preferred LLM specialisation and program based on your rank and available options.
  • Confirm your seat and pay the admission fee to secure your place in the program.

6. Documents Verification and Registration:

  • Submit all required documents for verification, including certificates, mark sheets, and admission confirmation letters.
  • Complete the university registration process and fulfil any additional formalities.

7. Orientation and Start of Programme:

  • Attend the university orientation program to meet faculty, fellow students, and familiarise yourself with the academic environment.
  • Enrol in your chosen LLM courses and embark on your journey towards higher specialisation in the legal field.

Direct LLM Admission Process Without Entrance Exam

While entrance exams are the most common route to LLM admission in India, some universities offer direct enrollment options without requiring exam scores. Here's what you need to know about the direct LLM admission process:

Check the Eligibility Criteria:

  • Hold a Bachelor's degree in Law (LLB) from a recognized university.
  • Meet the minimum percentage requirement (usually higher than entrance exam criteria, often 60% or above).
  • Some universities might have additional criteria like work experience, specific LLB specialisation, or outstanding academic performance.

Online Application Process:

  • Check the websites of universities offering direct admission pathways.
  • Download and fill out the application forms meticulously, providing academic transcripts, certificates, and other required documents.
  • Pay the application fee as applicable.

Selection Criteria:

Universities often consider a combination of factors for direct admission, including:

  1. Undergraduate Marks: Your LLB aggregate scores hold significant weight for the direct admission process for LLM courses.
  2. Work experience: Relevant professional experience in the legal field can be an advantage.
  3. Personal statement: A well-crafted statement highlighting your motivations, goals, and potential can boost your candidacy.
  4. Letters of recommendation: Strong recommendations from professors or legal professionals can add value to your application.
  5. University interview: Some universities might conduct interviews to assess your communication skills and legal aptitude.

Here’s a general LLM Syllabus to give you an idea about the topics taught in the above-mentioned LLM subjects:

  • Constitutional Law: In this subject, you'll explore its fundamental principles like federalism, separation of powers, and fundamental rights. 
  • Jurisprudence: Jurisprudence delves into the philosophical underpinnings, questioning its function, relationship with society, and different schools of legal thought. It's like peering into the mirror of law, reflecting on its essence and purpose.
  • Contract Law: Contract law equips you with the tools to understand how contracts are formed, interpreted, and enforced. Imagine it as mastering the language of agreements, ensuring fairness and clarity in every deal.
  • Tort Law: When someone suffers harm due to another's wrongful conduct, tort law provides the legal framework for seeking compensation and justice. Think of it as a shield that protects individuals from harm and ensures accountability.
  • Criminal Law: From understanding the elements of crimes to exploring criminal defences and punishment, criminal law delves into the complex world of criminal justice. Imagine navigating a maze of legal procedures and principles, seeking truth and ensuring a fair legal process.
  • Administrative Law: Government agencies play a crucial role in our lives, and administrative law scrutinises their rule-making and decision-making processes. Think of it as holding a magnifying glass to the actions of government agencies, ensuring they operate within the bounds of the law.
  • Public International Law: The world is interconnected, and public international law governs the relationships between sovereign states.

Beyond the core LLM subjects, you would be required to delve deeper into specific areas of legal interest through electives. Depending on your chosen specialisation, you could explore:

  • Business Law: Dive into the dynamic world of corporate transactions, mergers and acquisitions, and international trade. Imagine yourself navigating the fast-paced world of business deals and legal strategies.
  • Environmental Law: Become a champion for environmental protection by understanding legal frameworks and regulations that safeguard our planet. Imagine yourself advocating for clean air, water, and sustainable practices.
  • Family Law: Dive into the complexities of family dynamics, exploring issues like marriage, divorce, and child custody. Imagine navigating the sensitive legal terrain of family relationships with empathy and understanding.
  • Intellectual Property Law: Protect the fruits of creativity by studying patents, copyrights, trademarks, and trade secrets. Imagine yourself safeguarding the ideas and inventions that drive innovation and progress.
  • Human Rights Law: Fight for the fundamental rights of all individuals by engaging with international and domestic legal frameworks. Imagine yourself standing up for justice and equality for all, regardless of their background.

Is LLM better than MBA?
