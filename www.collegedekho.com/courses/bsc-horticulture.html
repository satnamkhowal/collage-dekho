BSc Horticulture Course

BSc Horticulture course is a three-year undergraduate programme that teaches the science of breeding vegetables, cereals, fruits, orchids, and other plants. The BSc Horticulture course covers subjects, including product marketing, farming management, pest control, and others. The BSc Horticulture syllabus covers all aspects of plants, from seed to fully grown plants, as well as how to care for them. The scientific knowledge provided aids their growth and productivity. The BSc Horticulture course incorporates many elements from chemistry, biology, biochemistry, genetic engineering, and other disciplines. Over the course of six semesters, students learn everything they need to know about plants.

The BSc Horticulture programme requires students to have completed their 10+2 in Science with Biology as a required subject. Candidates must have an overall class 12 average of at least 50% to be considered for this BSc Horticulture course; the percentage required varies depending on the BSc Horticulture college. BSc Horticulture admission 2024 is based on merit or entrance examination. Some universities require BSc Horticulture entrance exams at the state or university level though, BSc Horticulture programme does not have a national-level entrance examination. On the other hand, some BSc Horticulture colleges accept students based on their 10+2 exam results.

The BSc Horticulture course is offered by a number of specialised agricultural and horticultural universities including Govind Ballabh Pant University of Agriculture and Technology, Indira Gandhi Krishi Vishwavidyalaya, Shivaji University, and others, which are amongst the top BSc Horticulture colleges and universities.

The BSc Horticulture course fee often ranges between INR 15k - INR 50k.  The BSc Horticulture fees for distance education ranges from INR 30k – INR 80k. Some reputed universities provide grants in BSc Horticulture fees to reserved category students. Candidates who complete the course can work as an agricultural inspector, field officer, food inspector, agriculture business manager, conservation scientist, ground maintenance worker, ranch manager, soil and plant scientist, and make an average salary of INR 3 to INR 5 LPA.

List of Popular BSc Horticulture Specialisations

BSc in Horticulture allows you to select from an ample number of courses. These courses are also beneficial to those seeking a career in agriculture. They're listed below:

  • Floriculture: The maintenance, growth, and reproduction of ornamental flowers are all covered in this horticultural course. The development and breeding of new flower varieties through mating and other related techniques is also included in the field of floristry.
  • Sericulture: This course covers raising silkworms as well as producing different kinds of silks. In order to raise silkworms, you will learn about the different kinds of silkworms and their diet.
  • Apiculture: Apiculture is the process of raising and maintaining bees and their hives for the purpose of making products such as jelly, honey, and beeswax. The topic covers the different kinds of bee species, the variations in their yield, and their preservation.
  • Organic Farming: Organic farming is the practice of producing plants and raising animals naturally, without the use of artificial fertilisers or chemicals. Its goals are to preserve soil fertility and ecological balance while cutting down on waste and pollution.
  • Soil Science: The study of soil encompasses a variety of topics, such as land use, nutrients, mapping, soil inventory, edaphic factors, fertility, and classification.
  • Food Production and Technology: This area of study looks into how different garden tools and equipment, pesticides, and herbicides affect the production of food. Aspects of agriculture and food products that are technical, like tilling and irrigation, are also examined by food technology.

Types of BSc Horticulture

Here are some types of Horticulture:

  • Arboriculture: The cultivation of trees, shrubs, and other wood-based plants for ornamental purposes of experimentation.
  • Floriculture: The cultivation, processing, and marketing of ornamental plants.
  • Ornamental horticulture: The cultivation and use of decorative plants that generally have no food or manufacturing value.
  • Viticulture: The cultivation of grapes, most often for the purpose of wine making.
  • Agronomist: The study of plants and finding new ways to grow and modify them. Agronomists can also be called crop scientists.
  • Farmer: The planting, growing, harvesting, and selling of food-producing crops.

What is an Online BSc Horticulture Degree?

Online BSc Horticulture course is an undergraduate degree program that typically lasts three to four years. It focuses on the science and art of growing fruits, vegetables, flowers, ornamental plants, and other crops. The program includes courses on crop psychology, agricultural science, plant biotechnology, and agribusiness management. Horticulture degrees provide science-based education that can be applied to a wide range of careers, including landscape plant production and the use of plant materials in both constructed and natural landscapes. 

Candidates who cannot pursue BSc Horticulture Course in regular mode have the option to do so by opting for distance learning that allows students to study without leaving their current responsibilities. 

However, as of December 2023, the UGC and DEB have not approved any university to offer a BSc Horticulture distance education course in India; however, Colorado State University does offer an online horticulture degree with a concentration in horticultural business management.
