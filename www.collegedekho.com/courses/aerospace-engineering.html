Aerospace Engineering Course Overview

Aerospace engineering is the primary field of engineering involved with the development of aircraft and spacecraft. It has two significant and conjoining departments: aeronautical engineering and astronautical engineering. Avionics engineering is a parallel side of aerospace engineering but concerned with the electronics aspect of aerospace engineering. Candidates can pursue an aerospace engineering course after completing their Class 12th from the science stream from a recognized board. To pursue aerospace engineering candidates must have scored a valid percentage in class 12th and pass the entrance exams like JEE Main, JEE Advanced, SET, etc. 

Some of the top aerospace engineering recruiters are HAL, ISRO, DRDO, Airbnb, etc. The aerospace engineering courses are available in online and offline mode and are offered at the diploma level, bachelor’s, master's, and doctoral levels. Some of India's top aerospace engineering colleges are IIT Madras, IIT Bombay, Manipal Insitute of Technology, IIT Kharagpur, etc. And, the aerospace engineering fees in India range from INR 60,0000 LPA to INR 5,00,000 LPA for a bachelor’s degree. 

Quick Facts about Aerospace Engineering

  • As flight technology made progress in containing vehicles working in outer space, the more comprehensive term "aerospace engineering" has been used. Aerospace engineering, specifically the astronautics branch, is conversationally referred to as "rocket science". 
  • Aerospace engineering graduates can find career opportunities with governmental organizations, manufacturing companies, the International Space Station, or airlines.
  • After pursuing Aerospace Engineering, one can work as an Aerospace Engineer or Production Specialist in various private firms dealing with spare parts and production.
  • Aerospace Engineering is a superficially specialized technical field that requires overall excellence in calculative, mathematical, and observational skills.

Why Choose an Aerospace Engineering Degree?

With the advancement in the space sectors, the countries are growing their air force and looking forward to making their mark in space. There is a great need for space crafts, satellites, airspace instruments, etc. To cater to these needs several aerospace engineers are required making aerospace engineering a prospective profession. The following are the major reasons that you must choose an aerospace engineering course to study. 

Equip you with Skill

Aerospace Engineering will provide you with practical skills that are immediately applicable to the space sector and job. Because the aerospace sector is somewhat closed-off, a degree like this will provide you with the contacts and connections you need to establish your career.

Jobs Opportunities

Aerospace Engineering is a solid choice for a high-paying job since graduates are in great demand. Making planes and spaceships is a large industry, and employers are keen to acquire people with practical expertise. Graduates of this programme have a wide range of options for employment. Joining the RAF, Navy, or Army Air Corps, or developing vehicles for private corporations are just a few of the alternatives available.

Aid in Changing World

Aerospace Engineering is at the cutting edge of current technology, and a degree in this field might allow you to transform the world. 

Opportunity in ISRO

The ISRO, India's primary space agency, ranks fifth in the world with a budget of more than 1.5 billion dollars and a record of launching the most satellites in a single mission. It is not the end; numerous satellites, rockets, missiles, and other systems will be launched in the future, raising the ISRO's international profile and expanding job options subsequently opening several aerospace engineering jobs.

What is the Difference Between Aerospace Engineering and Aeronautical Engineering?

Many students are perplexed by the distinction between Aerospace Engineering and Aeronautical Engineering.  Aerospace Engineering, on the other hand, is concerned with the construction of both spacecraft and airplanes. Aeronautical engineering is concerned with the design of airplanes, helicopters, and aircraft.  There is a significant difference between these two engineering specializations, and the table below will help you understand why.

Basis of difference Aerospace engineeringAeronautical Engineering
MeaningAerospace engineering is concerned with the study of spacecraft that function both within and outside of the environment.Aeronautical Engineering includes study of aircraft that operate inside the atmosphere
Subjects

In aerospace engineering subjects include topics like Designing of Spacecraft, aircraft, missiles, or space stations, Fundamental Principles related to jet propulsion

Dynamics of spacecraft, Introduction to the field of Aeronautics, Introduction to Orbital Mechanics

In aeronautical engineering subjects include topics like Thermodynamics, Heat Transfer,  Flight Mechanics, Aircraft Structures, Aircraft Stability, and Control
Job OptionsThe aerospace job options consist of Aircraft/Spacecraft Designer, Military Aerospace Engineer, Inspector and Compliance Officer, etcThe aeronautical engineering jobs are of Racing Car Designer, Flight Mechanics Engineer, Graduate Engineer Trainee, Assistant aircraft engineer, etc

Aerospace Engineering Eligibility Criteria

If you are fascinated by how airplanes and aircraft are made and developed, as well as what the mechanism of flying airplanes is, aerospace engineering is the profession for you.  Engineers in the aerospace industry work on planes, space shuttles, satellites, and even weapons. To become an aerospace engineer, you should fulfill the following eligibility criteria 

Aerospace Engineering Eligibility Criteria for Diploma 

To get admitted to the diploma in the aerospace engineering programme, the candidate must have a minimum of 50% in your class 10th test, with science and math as mandatory courses

Aerospace Engineering Eligibility Criteria for Undergraduate Degree

Passing higher secondary or Class 12 with a minimum of 60% (55.5% for SC/ST) in Science is the minimum qualifying condition for pursuing a B.Tech Aerospace Engineering (Physics, Chemistry, and Maths)

Aerospace Engineering Eligibility Criteria for Master’s Degree

The majority of degree programmes in aerospace engineering are given at the post-graduate level. For this, the candidate must have a Bachelor's degree (BE/B Tech or equivalent) with a minimum of 60% (55 % for SC/ST).

GATE scores are necessary for entrance to postgraduate and doctoral programmes, as well as research.

Candidates with a three-four year engineering degree, a technical diploma, a five-year architecture degree, and a postgraduate degree in mathematics/science/statistics/computer applications are also qualified to study Aerospace Engineering

Aerospace Engineering Eligibility Criteria for NASA

If you want to work for the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) in the United States (US), you should know that NASA recommends a degree in Biomedical Engineering, Ceramic Engineering, Chemistry, Industrial Engineering, Materials Engineering, Metallurgy, Optical Engineering, and Oceanography.

Aerospace Engineering Entrance Exams

Admission to aerospace engineering courses is done mostly via the entrance examination. Candidates who want to pursue a bachelor's degree in aerospace engineering are required to sit for the entrance exam and qualify with flying colors scoring the required cutoff to get admission into the college. Some of the major aerospace engineering entrance exams are 

JEE Main

JEE Main is a computer-based online test administered by the National Testing Agency for students wishing to pursue an undergraduate degree at India's premier engineering colleges in various disciplines of engineering. 

JEE Advanced

The JEE Advanced, also known as the Joint Entrance Examination (Advanced), is the second stage of the IIT JEE main exam. Every year, one of seven Zonal IITs administers the test on behalf of JAB (Joint Admission Board). Candidates who pass both the JEE Main and Advanced will be admitted to the prestigious Indian Insitute of Technology (IITs) in India to study B.Tech in aerospace engineering.

GATE

The Graduate Aptitude Test in Engineering (GATE) is a national test that is approved and administered in eight districts across the country. The GATE Committee, faculty members from IISc, and seven other IITs administer the test on behalf of the Ministry of Human Resources Development, the National Coordinating Board, and the Department of Education.

Direct Aerospace Engineering Admission Process Without Entrance Exam

Some colleges and universities allow direct admission to their Aerospace Engineering programs based on your previous academic record. If you have scored well in your high school or previous undergraduate studies, you might be eligible for direct admission.

Another way to secure admission is through merit-based scholarships. If you excel in your academics and meet the scholarship criteria, you might get a seat in the Aerospace Engineering program without an entrance exam. If you are already enrolled in a related program or have completed some college coursework, transfer programs might work for you. These programs allow you to transfer into an Aerospace Engineering program after meeting certain educational requirements.

Your extracurricular activities and letters of recommendation can also play a significant role in your admission process. A strong record of extracurricular activities related to aeronautics or engineering and impressive letters of recommendation can make up for the absence of entrance exam scores. Your statement or essay can be a powerful tool in showcasing your dedication and enthusiasm for Aerospace Engineering. It could be the deciding factor for some institutions. Although rare, some colleges might conduct interviews during the admissions process for Aerospace Engineering. If you get an interview opportunity, take it as a chance to showcase both your qualifications and passion for the field.

Finally, some institutions offer fast-track programs for students who have already completed a relevant undergraduate degree in engineering or a related field. These direct entry programs can help you save time and get you on the path to your dream career in Aerospace Engineering faster.

Aerospace Engineering Syllabus and Subjects

Aerospace engineering is defined as a subset of engineering that focuses on the abilities required to design, construct, and repair airplanes and other spacecraft. It also covers weapons systems and missiles. As Aerospace Engineering is primarily concerned with mechanical elements, it likely overlaps with the mechanical engineering area. Aerospace Engineering, although covering a wide range of subjects, also incorporates computer science, physics, and mathematics applications. Therefore, students will become proficient in subjects like physics, mathematics, and science while studying the aerospace engineering syllabus. 

Electricity and robotics are two more topics that candidates will study in the Aerospace Engineering course. Aerospace Engineering also encompasses aeronautical engineering and astronautical engineering classes. As a result, it is often regarded as the most difficult branch of engineering among students.

The aerospace engineering syllabus consists of core subjects and elective subjects. Mechanics (Solids and Fluids),  Heat Transfer, Thermodynamics, and Materials Science are all part of the aerospace engineering syllabus. There are additional optional subjects available to assist in strengthening and broadening the knowledge foundation. There have been no significant modifications to the AE course syllabus.

  • Thermodynamics and Propulsion is concerned with the impact of work, heat, and energy on a system
  • Thermodynamics is also concerned with the observations of large-scale systems that deal with the macroscopic environment
  • When students study propulsion, they look at ways to develop airplanes that can create thrust and help the vehicle fly through the air
  • As a result, propulsion is concerned with the basic operation and design of propulsion vehicles in aerospace
Aerodynamics
  • This study looks at how gases interact with and impose forces on moving bodies. Air is the most common gas found on Earth's surface
  • Aerodynamics is concerned with the forces of drag and lift induced by air flowing over and around solid things
  • Aerodynamics is also distinct from gas dynamics in that it exclusively studies the behavior of air in the Earth's atmosphere, whereas gas dynamics studies the behavior of several gases

  • This is the study of how matter's mechanical behavior is exploited in the construction of an airplane.

Aerospace Engineer Job Profile

An aerospace engineer is responsible for the following duties:

When working on a project, aerospace engineers make sure that the finished airplane and component parts will be secure

They ensure that the projects adhere to the necessary quality standards and design method acceptance requirements

Aerospace engineers evaluate product designs to make sure they follow technical guidelines, consumer needs, and environmental consideration

Aerospace engineers examine faulty or damaged goods to determine the cause of problems and offer suggestions for potential fixes

They develop cutting-edge technologies for use in aerospace, defense, and space exploration. Aerospace engineers are experts in a variety of fields, including robotics, propulsion, combustion, guidance, and structural design

The building of aircraft, as well as the installation of instruments, engines, and other hardware, is under the control of aerospace engineers

FAQs about BTech AE

Does ISRO hire aerospace engineers?

The Indian Space Research Organization (ISRO) hires candidates from the aerospace industry.
 

What is the eligibility for aerospace?

For pursuing aerospace engineering courses, candidates have to complete class 10+2 or its equivalent in the science stream (Physics, Chemistry, and Mathematics are compulsory subjects) with a minimum of 60% marks (General) or 55% marks (SC/ST/OBC) categories.

What should I study to become an Aerospace Engineer?

You have to complete your graduation in BTech Aerospace Engineering followed by MTech in Aerospace Engineering to become a successful Aerospace Engineer.
 

What is Aerospace Engineering course?

Aerospace engineering is largely used for the construction, design, and maintenance of  missiles, aircraft, weapons systems, and spacecraft. The main focus of Aerospace engineering is to include flight safety, fuel efficiency, operating costs, and environmental impact.
 

How many years is aerospace engineering?

 The duration of Aerospace Engineering is Two to Four years.
 

Do aerospace engineers fly the flights?

No. Pilots acquire experience by experiencing flying courses and they train for hundreds of hours, whereas aerospace engineers are accountable for the design, maintenance, and overall supervision of aircraft. 
 

Does NASA recruit from IIT?

Although NASA only recruits scientists and other professionals who are citizens of the United States of America (USA), it does not mean that all doors are closed for Indian students to become NASA scientists after the completion of Aerospace Engineering.
 

Can I do Aerospace Engineering without IIT?

Yes, candidates who have passed or appeared in class 12th with PCM or PCB with 75%+ are eligible to get admission in aerospace engineering.
 

Who are the top recruiters of Aerospace Engineering Graduates?

National Aerospace Laboratories (NAL), Defense Research And Development Organization (DRDO), Taneja Aerospace, Indian Space Research Organizations (ISRO), and L&T are some of the top recruiters of Aerospace Engineering Graduates.
 

What are the job options available after concluding Aerospace Engineering Course?

Data Processing Manager, Data Analyst, Aircraft mechanic, Maintenance Engineer, and Mechanical Engineer are some of the job options available after concluding Aerospace Engineering Course.
 

What is the average salary of an Aerospace Engineering Graduate in India?

In India, an average graduating aeronautical engineer may make roughly INR 45,000 per month.
 

What is the average salary of an Aerospace Designer Checker?

The average salary of an Aerospace Designer Checker is Rs. 30 lakhs - Rs. 50 lakhs per annum
 

Is aeronautical in demand?

Yes, aeronautical engineering is a very in-demand degree as we are in a technological era and several aircraft are needed for cargo, traveling, military, etc for which there is a huge requirement for skilled aeronautical engineers.

Can I do aerospace after Class 12th?

Yes, you can pursue aerospace engineering after completing Class 12th from a recognized board with a science stream. 

 

Which course is best for aerospace engineering?

Some of the best aerospace engineering courses are BE/ BTech in Aerospace Engineering, BS/ BS(Hons) in Aerospace Engineering, MS in Aerospace Engineering, ME/MTech in Aerospace Engineering, PhD in Aerospace Engineering, etc. 

 

Is an aerospace engineering course hard to pursue?

Yes, an aerospace-engaging degree is hard to pursue, but they also provide some of the finest possibilities after graduation. Aerospace engineers must have a high level of technical proficiency and accuracy, as well as the ability to think quickly and creatively.

 

Does NASA hire aerospace engineers?

Yes, NASA hires aerospace engineers. NASA employs 20 distinct sorts of engineers to work on its numerous tasks; the aerospace, general, and computer engineering sectors are the most popular.

 

Is aerospace engineering a good career?

Given how quickly the aviation and space industries are expanding, competent aerospace engineers will undoubtedly find a wide range of employment prospects. One of the top five engineers with the highest salaries is an aerospace engineer.

 

Is aerospace engineering a good salary?

An experienced aerospace engineer with 10–20 years of experience gets an average pay of 13.1 lakhs per year, compared to mid-career aerospace engineers with 4–9 years of experience.

 

What does aerospace engineering do?

Aerospace engineers work in fields where people develop or construct spacecraft, missiles, national security systems, or airplanes. They typically work for the federal government and businesses involved in manufacturing, research and development, analysis, and design.
