BSc in Psychology programme is a three-year bachelor's degree that offers a deep dive into the science of mind and human behaviour. The course deals with core areas such as developmental psychology, cognitive psychology, neuropsychology, social dynamics and social behaviour. Theoretical knowledge with practical experience through laboratory work, research projects, and internships are part of the curriculum. The individual with a passion for understanding human behaviour and a desire to pursue a career or further studies in psychology can pursue the course. 

BSc Psychology Course

BSc Psychology is a three-year undergraduate program that focuses on the human mind and behavior. It is the Psychology degree that combines psychology and fundamental cognitive science to provide students with a thorough understanding of human nature. The BSc Psychology syllabus and subjects are designed to provide students with both theoretical and practical knowledge of the subject. The most common BSc Psychology subjects are Stress and Well-Being, Memory, Attention and Perception, Psychology of Individual Differences, Statistical Methods for Psychological Research, Biopsychology, and Introduction to Psychology.

To be eligible for the BSc Psychology admission process, students must have passed their 10+2 or equivalent exams from a recognised board with at least a 50% overall average marks secured. Presidency College in Chennai, Christ University in Chandigarh, Sister Nivedita University in Kolkata, Jain University in Bangalore, and others are among the BSc Psychology Colleges in India. The average BSc Psychology course fee ranges from INR 20 K to INR 60 K. Psychologists, Child Psychology Assistants, Mental Health Counsellors, Industrial Psychologists, and other BSc psychology jobs are available in the public and private sectors.

Candidates with a BSc in Psychology can further their education and gain experience for a career as a Psychologist by pursuing an MSc in Psychology. Candidates for BSc Psychology jobs can work as Motivators, Counsellors, Health Educators, School Psychologists, Forensic Psychologists, Community Relations Officers, and Counsellors, in addition to Psychologists. A BSc Psychology salary can range between INR 4 and 5 LPA on average. Candidates may also apply to programmes similar to the BSc Psychology, such as BSc Clinical Psychology, BSc Hons Psychology, BA in Psychology, and others. 

Studying a BSc Psychology course provides a thorough understanding of human behaviour and mental processes, which can be applied in a variety of settings. Candidates will develop their interpersonal skills and empathy. It also develops critical thinking and problem-solving abilities, which are in high demand in various kinds of professions.

BSc Psychology Eligibility Criteria

For admission into BSc Psychology course, the University Grants Commission (UGC) has set specific eligibility criteria. The below-mentioned criteria are common for most universities and colleges across the country that offer a BSc Psychology degree.

  • Candidates are required to pass the 10+2 or equivalent examination from a recognized board with a minimum aggregate of 50% marks
  • Candidates should pass the qualifying examination in the Science stream with biology as a mandatory subject
  • During BSc Psychology admission, the candidate should be at least 17 years old years and less than 35 years
  • Candidates should have cleared all the subjects at the time of admission. The supplementary exam in any of the subjects at 10+2 or equivalent level will not be considered
  • Apart from that, candidates are required to clear the standard and university-level entrance exams with a required grade or scores to get admission to premier colleges
  • If a candidate is applying with reservation criteria, then he/she should fulfill the additional eligibility criteria to complete the admission process
  • All the documents required at the time of admission should be original and as per the latest formats

What is the Difference Between BSc Psychology and BSc Sociology?

Here is a simple way to understand the distinction between psychology and sociology. Sociology covers both collective society and particular social identity groups like gender, race, and social class. On the other hand, psychology focuses on the individual but also takes into account group dynamics, such as workplace behaviours.

A programme in research methods, social identity groups, and behavioural analysis is not that simple, regardless of the field you choose. You can get ready for a variety of career options by earning a bachelor's degree in psychology or sociology, for example. The distinctions between a BSc in psychology and a BSc in sociology are as follows:

BSc PsychologyBSc Sociology
This course is only about the person or individual.This programme addresses both a societal level and specific social identity groups such as gender, race, and social class.
The Psychology course uses measurements, observation, and analysis to determine the cause of human behaviour on a personal level.Sociology programmes examine culture, groups, organizations, and social institutions' social behavior.
The course's fundamental requirements are statistics, research methods, and behavioural analysis.This course asks us to look at the world through the eyes of different cultures and communities.
There will be opportunities in sales, criminal justice, social service, and public administration.Public, human, or social services, social research and analytics, social justice-related service, and human resources will be available as career options.

Career after BSc Psychology

To pursue a career in psychology after completing a BSc Psychology, students must first possess the previously mentioned basic qualities. The higher a person's degree, the more career options they have in any field. Theory and practice go hand in hand in the field of psychology, and with a growing demand for practitioners, a career in psychology is highly rewarding. Counsellors can work as psychologists in universities, hospitals, schools, clinics, government institutions, and other settings. Professional psychologists are in high demand these days, due to the rise in mental health concerns. 

Here are some popular careers and jobs options for BSc Psychology graduates:

Job ProfileDescription
PsychologistA Psychologist talks to patients and deals with their mental health and other issues. They study human behaviour and develop treatment plans. They do research work for the treatment of certain mental conditions. The functions of a psychologist depends on the specialty of the psychologist. Generally, psychologists conduct evaluations and psychological testing to diagnose patients. They provide what is referred to as "talk therapy." Psychologists provide a safe environment to express one's feelings. They are also bound by doctor-patient confidentiality, except when future harm to self or other is mentioned.
Human ResourcePsychology is all about understanding people and how they think, making human resources another good match for BSc Psychology students. This role is available in both the public and private sectors, include areas such as employee satisfaction, professional development, training, recruitment, PR, payroll, etc.
CounsellorAs a counsellor you will be involved in helping people come to better terms with their lives and experiences through exploration of feelings and emotions. You’ll work within a confidential setting and be expected to listen attentively to your clients. Key traits of a counsellor include the ability to listen, empathize, offer respect and patience, as well as to analyze the issues at play in order to enable the client to better cope with their situation and help support them in making choices. 
TeacherHaving a bachelor's degree in psychology and a teaching certificate is a great combination for becoming a teacher. BSc Psychology graduates can find jobs teaching at elementary schools, middle schools or even at high schools. They may teach various subject matters, including psychology.
Social PsychologistA social worker is someone who works with people who are going through difficult periods in their lives; including groups such as children or the elderly, people with disabilities and victims of crime and abuse. The role of a social worker is to safeguard these people from harm and provide support in order to allow people to improve their situations.
