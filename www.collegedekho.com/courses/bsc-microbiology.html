BSc Microbiology Course Overview

BSc Microbiology is the best and well-chosen course in the Microbiology stream. A BSc Microbiology is an undergraduate degree program that focuses on the study of microorganisms, including bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa, and other microscopic life forms. BSc Microbiology (Bachelor of Science in Microbiology) is a three-year degree programme that studies and researches microorganisms such as Bacteria, Viruses, Fungi, Protozoa, and others, as well as their effects on the human body. It is primarily concerned with Microorganisms, which include organisms such as Unicellular Organisms, Clusters of Microscopic Animals, Viruses, Bacteria, and Fungi. Microbiology is used in both biochemistry and pathology research.

In order to be eligible for BSc in Microbiology candidates are required to have completed Class 12th or its equivalent from a recognized board of education with Physics, Chemistry, and Biology (PCM) as mandatory subjects. BSc Microbiology is provided in offline mode across numerous educational institutes located across the country. Some of the major topics covered in the BSc Microbiology syllabus and subjects are Molecular Ecology, Enzymes Technology, Molecular biology, Industrial Microbiology, Immunology among others.  

Most top colleges offer BSc Microbiology admission 2024 based on merit or performance in entrance exams like CUET and LPUNEST, among others. St. Xavier's College, Jai Hind College, Ramnarain Ruia Autonomous College, Kishinchand Chellaram College, and others are among the most popular colleges that offer the programme. The annual fee for a BSc Microbiology course ranges between INR 20K and INR 50K. Candidates who complete the course may find BSc Microbiology jobs in Forensic Science Laboratories, Healthcare Organisations, Environmental Organisations, Pharmaceuticals, Food and Beverage, Higher Education Institutions, Publicly Funded Research Organisations, and a wide range of other industries.

Depending on the candidate's experience, the average annual starting salary for BSc Microbiology graduates in India ranges between INR 3 and 10 LPA. Candidates interested in furthering their education can pursue courses after BSc Microbiology such as MSc Microbiology, MSc Applied Microbiology, MSc Medical Microbiology, and MSc Microbial Genetics and Bioinformatics.

BSc Microbiology Admission through CUET

Christ University Entrance Test (CUET) is one of the most popular entrance exams for admission to a BSc Microbiology program. Students who meet the eligibility criteria must apply online and take the CUET. Students who score well in the CUET will have to appear for an interview round. 

To be eligible for admission to a BSc Microbiology program, students must:

  • Have completed 10+2 with Physics, Biology, and Chemistry as core subjects
  • Have scored at least 50% overall (45% for SC/ST candidates)
  • Have scored at least 60% in each core subject 

BSc Microbiology Admission through Common University Entrance Test (CUET UG)

To be eligible for a Bachelor of Science (BSc) in Microbiology, students must have passed their 10+2 in the science stream with a minimum of 50% marks. Students may also need to clear entrance exams such as the Common University Entrance Test (CUET UG). 

The minimum eligibility requirements for BSc Microbiology are:

  • Pass Xth and XIIth with Physics, Biology, and Chemistry as core subjects
  • Minimum 50% overall (45% for SC/ST candidates)
  • 60% in each core subject
  • Candidates must have passed their 12th or equivalent exam
  • At least 55-60% aggregate marks in Science Stream (Physics, Chemistry, Biology)

Career After BSc Microbiology

In the fields of medical research, quality control, and the biotechnology industry, microbiologists are in high demand today. Many graduates find employment as researchers in academic institutions, pharmaceutical and bioscience businesses and organisations. Graduates of the BSc Microbiology programme also advance in clinical or technical laboratory-based careers without a focus on original research. Those who want to work in the field of microbiology have many job options at their disposal. Graduates in microbiology will be able to work in a variety of fields, including government and private hospitals, the food industry, research and development institutions, the chemical industry, and the pharmaceutical industry.

Graduates of BSc Microbiology can also choose to work as science writers who produce content for working microbiologists. Microbiologists' pay is based on a number of factors, including their education, experience, and job description.

What three roles does a microbiologist perform?

Microbiologists research different kinds of parasites, bacteria, viruses, algae, and fungi. They make an effort to comprehend how these organisms function, develop, and engage with their surroundings.

Is microbiology difficult?

Microbiology is a difficult subject to master. It's very detailed, and you'll need to remember a lot of information about microscopic organisms, morphologies, and modes of action. You will most likely struggle if you do not have some basic knowledge of biology and chemistry, as well as the ability to memorise things quickly.
 

What exactly are the two types of microbiology?

There are two types of microbiology: pure and applied. The former is the most fundamental branch, in which organisms are thoroughly examined. The organisms themselves are not studied in applied microbiology; rather, they are applied to a specific process.
