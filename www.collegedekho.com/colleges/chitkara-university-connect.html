The Portfolio Every Finance Professional Forgets They Own

Every finance professional knows the first rule of managing money: nothing sits untouched. Weightings drift as markets move, and a mix that made sense three years ago quietly stops making sense today. So the discipline is to review, trim, add, rebalance — not because something broke, but because inaction is itself a bet, and usually a losing one.

That discipline gets applied everywhere in the job. Client portfolios, treasury positions, loan books, risk exposures — all of it sits on a review cycle. Nothing is allowed to drift unexamined. Except, oddly, the asset that made all of that other reviewing possible in the first place: the professional's own expertise. Skills sharpened at thirty are still being relied on at forty-five, untouched, as if professional competence were somehow immune to the same repricing everything else on the balance sheet goes through.

It isn't immune. It may be the most volatile holding a finance professional carries — and right now, it's being repriced faster than most people have noticed.

Two Directions of Value

Look at what's happened inside a typical finance skill set. Ten years ago, building a clean model or tearing apart a company's numbers was a scarce, well-paid skill. Automation has since pulled a lot of that mechanical work out of human hands, and with it, the market has quietly marked down the value of pure technical execution. Getting faster at it doesn't undo that shift.

Meanwhile, a different set of capabilities is climbing in value. Knowing which analysis is worth running in the first place. Standing between a data team and a boardroom and making each side legible to the other. Recognizing when a confident model deserves trust and when it deserves a challenge. Treating a regulatory or ethical boundary as part of the decision's design rather than a hurdle to clear. None of this is new — but it's now what separates the professional who directs the machines from the one competing against them.

That's the repricing happening inside individual careers: the technical floor is turning into a commodity while judgement above it becomes the scarce asset. A professional still weighted toward the first and light on the second is carrying a portfolio badly out of balance — whether or not the paycheck has caught up to that reality yet.

Nobody Rebalances by Accident

The comfortable assumption is that this corrects itself with enough time on the job. It doesn't, usually. Daily work has a habit of deepening the skills you already have rather than building the ones you're missing — which is exactly how portfolios drift in the first place. Fixing that takes the same deliberateness as fixing any other allocation: putting time behind the capabilities gaining value, before the job market forces the correction on worse terms.

This is precisely the gap a targeted management education is built to close for someone who assumed their classroom years were over. Chitkara University's Centre for Distance and Online Education offers an Online MBA in Finance built around this exact rebalancing — strategic thinking, cross-functional fluency, the shift from operating inside a finance function to leading one. Doing it online isn't incidental; it's what makes the shift possible without stepping off the income and experience track a full-time program would require.

The Fastest-Appreciating Skill on the Sheet

One capability deserves its own line item, because nowhere is the repricing sharper. Financial decisions increasingly run through systems built on artificial intelligence — credit scoring, fraud detection, algorithmic trading, robo-advisory, forecasting. Everyone will soon know how to operate these tools. Far fewer will understand them well enough to question them, govern them, and decide where they should and shouldn't be trusted with a decision. That's the group that ends up leading finance over the next decade.

That kind of fluency is still genuinely rare — which is why Chitkara University's Online MBA in Finance pairs financial management with a Minor in Artificial Intelligence in Business. The goal isn't to turn finance professionals into engineers. It's to make sure that when a model spits out an answer, there's someone in the room who understands exactly where that answer stops being reliable. It's an asset likely to keep appreciating and unlikely to be automated away — and getting in early is the same logic as buying good value before the wider market notices.

Credibility Is Part of the Asset

No finance professional needs reminding that a holding is only worth what the market will trust it for. The same logic applies to a credential — rebalancing only works if the qualification behind it carries weight where it matters. That's why the institution isn't a footnote here. Chitkara University is UGC-recognised with a long track record in industry-facing education, and its online degrees carry UGC entitlement — the same standing as their on-campus equivalents for employment and further study. That's not marketing language; it's what makes the rebalance count in the eyes of the market it's meant for.

None of this asks a finance professional to abandon a discipline they already trust. It just asks them to point that same discipline inward, at the one holding usually left off the review.

The Review That Never Gets Scheduled

There's a quiet irony in a profession built around revaluing everything except the person doing the valuing. The same professional who'd never let a client sit on a decade-old, undiversified position without a hard conversation will often hold exactly that position in their own career — and call it stability. The fix is the same one they'd recommend to anyone else: look honestly at what's appreciating and what isn't, and start moving toward the first while it's still your decision to make, not the market's. Expertise was never a fixed endowment. Like everything else on the sheet, it's due for a review — ideally before someone else runs it for you.

Chitkara University signs MoU with Applus+ for Industry-Led Learning and Research

Chitkara University has signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with Applus+. The partnership establishes a framework for learning, industry engagement and research.
The partnership will give students opportunities to gain experience beyond the classroom through internships, industrial training, industry-led projects, technical workshops, expert interactions and skill development initiatives.

For students, the association is intended to provide a closer understanding of how academic knowledge is applied in professional settings. Through projects, training and interactions with industry professionals, they will have opportunities to work with practical problems and become familiar with the expectations of the workplace.

The MoU was signed by Dr. Cavita Taragi, Pro Vice Chancellor, Career Advancement Services and Head of Placements & Industry Partnerships, Chitkara University, and Francesc Pascual, Managing Director, Applus+ IDIADA.

The collaboration will create several points of interaction between students and Applus+ professionals. Industrial training and internships will allow students to gain exposure to professional environments, while industry-led projects can give them an opportunity to work on problems that are closer to those encountered in practice.

Technical workshops and expert sessions will add another dimension to this experience.Students will be able to hear directly from professionals about developments in their fields, the tools and methods used in industry, and the kinds of challenges that arise while working on real projects.

These interactions can also help students understand the connection between the concepts they encounter during their academic programmes and their application in professional work.

The partnership will not be limited to student engagement. It will also provide opportunities for faculty members and industry experts to work together on research, consultancy, knowledge exchange and innovation.

This can create scope for faculty to bring industry perspectives into academic discussions while also allowing industry professionals to engage with academic expertise and ongoing research. Research and consultancy projects can provide a further avenue for the two sides to work together on specific areas of interest.

Such engagement can help create projects where academic knowledge and practical experience inform each other, while giving students opportunities to observe or participate in work that goes beyond conventional coursework.

The collaboration also opens up opportunities for continued knowledge exchange.
As technology, professional practices and industry requirements change, regular interaction with practitioners can help students and faculty remain connected to developments taking place outside the university.

Commenting on the collaboration, Dr. Madhu Chitkara, President and Co-Founder, Chitkara University, said, “Industry engagement is an important part of preparing students for the realities of professional practice. This association with Applus+ provides opportunities for students and faculty to learn from industry experience while also creating scope for academic and professional knowledge to come together through projects, training and research.”

For students, one of the key aspects of the partnership will be the range of ways in which they can engage with industry.

Not every student learns in the same way, and exposure can take different forms. An internship offers experience of a professional workplace. A technical workshop can focus on a particular skill or area of practice. An industry project can require students to work through a problem from start to finish.

Expert interactions, meanwhile, can offer students a chance to understand the thinking and experience behind professional decisions. Together, these activities can give students a broader view of what their academic learning can lead to once they enter the workplace.

The partnership will also create opportunities for students to develop skills that are difficult to build through classroom learning alone. Working on projects with practical requirements can involve communication, teamwork, problem-solving, time management and adapting to changing situations. These experiences can complement the technical and academic knowledge students gain through their programmes.

For faculty members, the association provides a route for continued engagement with professionals working in the field. This can support knowledge exchange and create opportunities to explore research questions, consultancy projects and areas where academic expertise can be applied to industry requirements.

The MoU therefore establishes a framework that can support engagement at several levels — from student training and projects to faculty research and professional collaboration.The association between Chitkara University and Applus+ is expected to develop through continued interaction among students, faculty members and industry experts.

As activities under the partnership take shape, students will have opportunities to participate in training, projects, workshops and professional interactions, while faculty members can explore avenues for research, consultancy and knowledge exchange. The collaboration brings academic and industry perspectives into the same space, with the focus extending across learning, professional practice and research.

Chitkara University looks forward to building on the association with Applus+ through continued opportunities for students and faculty to learn, work and engage with industry.

Chitkara University and York University Sign MoU for 2+2 Computer Science Programme

Chitkara University has signed a Memorandum of Understanding with York University in Toronto, Canada, to introduce a 2+2 undergraduate programme in Computer Science. Under this MoU, students will complete the first two years of their Computer Science programme at Chitkara University, followed by the last two years at Lassonde School of Engineering at York University. On successful completion of the programme, students will be awarded a Bachelor of Science degree in Computer Science from York University.

University officials said the curriculum for the initial two years will be aligned with York University’s academic requirements to facilitate credit transfer, subject to eligibility criteria.  The authorities from both institutions attended the MoU signing ceremony, including Dr. Rhonda Lenton, President and Vice-Chancellor of York University, and Dr. Madhu Chitkara, Pro-Chancellor of Chitkara University. This programme will allow students to begin their studies in India before completing the final phase in Canada. Students transferring to York University may also have access to co-op opportunities as part of their academic programme. The first batch of students under the 2+2 pathway is expected to join York University in September 2028, subject to meeting admission and progression requirements.

            The Marketing Skills That Got You Promoted Aren't Always the Ones That Take You Further

            There's a particular kind of confusion that hits marketers somewhere around year eight or ten. Not burnout, exactly. Something quieter than that. You're still good at the job, arguably better than you've ever been. Campaigns land. The numbers hold up fine. You could probably run a channel half asleep at this point. And yet the promotion that used to show up like clockwork just stops showing up. The role you had your eye on goes to someone else instead, and nobody really sits you down to explain why.

            Here's what nobody tells you early enough in a marketing career. Getting good at the job and getting promoted for it are basically the same thing, for a while. Then one day, without any warning, they stop being the same thing. Almost nobody catches the moment it happens.

            Look at what actually gets rewarded in those first five or six years. Running a campaign that performs. Writing copy that converts. Keeping a client happy when things could easily fall apart. Reading a dashboard and knowing, right away, what to do with what it's telling you. These are craft skills, and they're hard won. Getting genuinely sharp at them is what gets a person noticed in the first place.

            Here's where it gets tricky, though. The better someone gets at that kind of work, the more of it keeps landing on their desk. They turn into the person everyone trusts to just handle things. Their calendar fills up with more of what they've already mastered, and somewhere inside all that capable, busy, reliable output, the next set of skills quietly stops getting built.

            The job shifts underneath them without much warning. Somewhere between executive and manager, and then again between manager and senior manager, the whole shape of the role changes. It stops being about how to run the campaign and starts being about whether the campaign should even run, and how anyone would know that. It stops being about what the dashboard says and becomes about what to actually do with that information, and what it might cost somewhere else in the business. Suddenly there's a budget to defend in a room full of finance people who couldn't care less how clever the creative is. There's an expectation to understand customers deeply enough to shape strategy, not just point an ad in their direction. There's a need to connect marketing spend to real revenue and say so out loud, to people who think in spreadsheets rather than campaigns.

            None of that is simply a harder version of what got someone here. It's an entirely different skill, one that has more to do with running a business than running a campaign, and it doesn't show up just from doing more of what already works.

            Some of why this gap feels sharper today comes down to how much marketing itself has expanded. Acquisition and retention used to be separate conversations. Now they sit at the same table as brand and performance. Digital sits with traditional. Hard analytics sit alongside the kind of consumer instinct no spreadsheet can quite capture. It's essentially one job now, where it used to be closer to five. Marketing decisions reach into pricing, product, the whole customer experience, in ways that simply weren't true a decade back. Senior marketers are expected to hold their ground in rooms that used to belong entirely to finance and operations.

            Which is really the honest case for going back to school, assuming someone's going to do it at all. Not for more marketing knowledge, since most people at that stage already have plenty. The case is for the broader stuff, the strategy, the finance, the leadership, the general business fluency that daily campaign work simply doesn't teach no matter how long someone stays in it. A well built MBA in Marketing sits right at that intersection.

            Walking away from a working career, a steady income and years of built up momentum to sit in a classroom full time isn't something most people want to do, and that's fair. It's exactly why so many capable marketers never go back to study at all. It's also why the online format actually matters here, rather than being treated as some lesser version of the real thing. Chitkara University's Centre for Distance and Online Education runs its Online MBA in Marketing so the degree can fit around a career already underway, instead of replacing it.

            Recognition is worth being blunt about too, because a degree that carries no weight isn't worth anyone's time regardless of format. Chitkara University is a UGC recognised institution, and its online degrees are UGC entitled, holding the same standing as the on-campus version for jobs and further study. That's not a small detail tucked at the bottom. For someone putting in real time and money to move up, it's often the entire reason the decision makes sense.

            The marketers who actually end up in those senior rooms tend to be the ones who notice this shift a little early and start preparing before the role they wanted has already gone to somebody else. No degree hands anyone a promotion outright. But the job someone's aiming for wants a different set of proof than the job they're currently in, and the people making that call are usually looking for signs of business thinking, not just campaign thinking.

            Everything that built a career up to this point still matters, and it always will. The real question is whether it's enough on its own for what comes next. Sometimes the biggest move isn't a new company or a bigger title. Sometimes it's just deciding to build the next set of skills before the job demands them. For marketers ready to make that move without stepping off the career they've already built, the Online MBA in Marketing at Chitkara University's Centre for Distance and Online Education offers exactly that.
